\section{\black{Experiments}}
\label{sec:experiments}
\black{
We evaluate our system on multiple dimensions: reconstruction quality, tracking performance, and line-of-sight accuracy.}

\subsection{\black{Evaluation Metrics}}
\black{
\textbf{Static Scene Quality:} Point cloud density, multi-view consistency, camera pose stability errors measured.).

\textbf{Per-Camera Tracking Quality:} Per-camera detection continuity, movement classification, track ID consistency, 3D trail accuracy.

\textbf{Multi-Camera Association Quality:} Cross-camera association precision/recall, spatial alignment of merged tracks, temporal consistency of global object IDs.

\textbf{3D Primitive Quality:} Spatial alignment of primitives with triangulated track positions, primitive scale accuracy.

\textbf{Line-of-Sight Metrics:} Visibility score over time (fraction of vehicle visible), first visible frame detection, occluder identification accuracy.}

\subsection{\black{Baselines}}
\black{
To demonstrate the value of our multi-view 3D approach, we compare against:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-camera only:} Tracking without multi-view fusion
    \item \textbf{No static scene:} Dynamic objects only, no environmental occlusion reasoning
    \item \textbf{2D visibility test:} Simple image-plane bounding box overlap test
\end{itemize}}

\subsection{\black{Hypothesis}}
\black{A full multi-camera 3D pipeline with Pi3 static geometry + fused dynamic object primitives will provide more accurate and earlier Line-of-Sight detection than any 2D or single-camera baseline. The combination of multi-view cues, static scene geometry, and 3D primitive representation enables correct occluder reasoning that 2D methods cannot achieve.}

\subsection{\blue{Experimental Results}}
\blue{
We evaluate our two-pass 3D reconstruction pipeline on the StreetAware dataset across six experiments covering static reconstruction, per-camera tracking, multi-camera association, 3D primitive accuracy, LoS visibility, and baseline comparisons.}

\subsubsection{\black{Static Scene Reconstruction}}
\black{
We first evaluate the quality of the static scene reconstruction produced in Pass 1. The system reconstructs a dense point cloud containing approximately 1.34 million points, corresponding to a high spatial density of approximately 6.1 × 106 points per cubic meter. The coverage fraction is 1.0, indicating full reconstruction of the roadway and crosswalk region. Although the mean reprojection error is high (greater than 1000 pixels) due to imperfect camera calibration and residual dynamic points, the mean visible fraction (approximately 0.32) confirms partial multi-view consistency. Figure 3 shows the dense reconstructed static scene. Overall, the static scene provides a complete geometric backbone for downstream visibility reasoning.}



\subsubsection{\blue{Per-Camera Object Tracking}}
\blue{
We evaluated per-camera object tracking using YOLOv8 and
ByteTrack across all eight synchronized views. Each cam
era yields between 20 and 40 object tracks, with median
track lengths ranging from 30 to 70 frames (approximately
2–5 seconds) and low fragmentation. Track completeness
is high, indicating stable temporal association. Using 3D
velocity thresholds, objects are reliably classified as stationary or moving, enabling correct identification of parked vehicles and dynamic agents. This experiment confirms that per-camera tracking provides robust single-view motion summaries suitable for multi-camera fusion.}

\subsubsection{\blue{Multi-Camera Association}}
\blue{
{Union-find clustering merges tracks from multiple cameras
viewing the same object. Metrics: precision >90% (low
false merge rate) and recall >85% (accounting for objects
visible to only one camera).}}

\subsubsection{\black{3D Primitive Representation and Scale Calibration}}
\black{
Global objects are modeled using canonical 3D primitives motion-aligned bounding boxes for vehicles and vertical cylinders for pedestrians with reconstructed tracks closely aligning to these shapes (Figure 4). Scene scale is recovered via pedestrian height normalization (median 1.7 m), vehicle dimensions match real-world sizes (~4.5 m), and high inside-point ratios (~1.0 vehicles, ~0.95 pedestrians) confirm strong geometric consistency.}

\subsubsection{\blue{Line-of-Sight Visibility Analysis}}
\blue{
We evaluate LoS visibility via ray-casting from pedestrian eye points to vehicle keypoints through the combined static and dynamic scene. Most pedestrian–vehicle pairs show visibility fractions near 1.0, first visibility at frame 0, and no occlusions, as expected in the open intersection geometry; when present, occlusions are correctly attributed to static or dynamic objects. Table 1 summarizes the statistics, and overall results validate correct LoS pipeline behavior under realistic urban conditions.}

\subsubsection{\black{Baseline Comparisons}}
\black{Compared to a single-camera baseline, our multi-view 3D LoS method detects approaching vehicles an average of 175 frames (~12.16 s) earlier across 15 pedestrian trajectories. Visibility duration is similar, confirming that full-scene 3D reasoning enables earlier and more accurate conflict-relevant perception rather than longer visibility.}
