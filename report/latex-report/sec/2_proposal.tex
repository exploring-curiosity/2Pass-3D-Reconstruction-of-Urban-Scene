\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Intersection Safety and Visibility Analysis}

Traditional visibility audits at urban intersections rely on manual inspection or simplified 2D geometric analysis following guidelines such as the AASHTO Green Book~\cite{aashto2018greenbook}. These methods assess sight triangles and stopping sight distances but cannot capture complex 3D occlusions from dynamic objects. Recent studies have highlighted limited sight distance as a significant contributing factor in pedestrian fatalities~\cite{nhtsa2023pedestrian,lord2007safety}. Automated video-based safety analysis has emerged as a promising alternative~\cite{saunier2010automated}, though most approaches remain limited to 2D trajectory analysis without explicit 3D reasoning.

\subsection{Multi-View 3D Reconstruction}

Multi-view stereo (MVS) methods estimate dense 3D geometry from multiple calibrated camera views. Classical approaches like COLMAP~\cite{schoenberger2016sfm} combine Structure-from-Motion (SfM) for camera pose estimation with MVS for dense reconstruction. Learning-based methods including MVSNet~\cite{yao2018mvsnet} and its variants predict depth maps from cost volumes constructed across views.

Recent advances have unified pose estimation and reconstruction. DUSt3R~\cite{dust3r} uses a transformer architecture to jointly estimate camera poses and dense point maps from uncalibrated image pairs, enabling reconstruction from casually captured images. MASt3R~\cite{mast3r} extends this with improved matching. Pi3~\cite{pi3} further advances multi-view reconstruction with enhanced global optimization. We adopt Pi3 for its strong performance on outdoor multi-view scenarios without requiring pre-calibrated cameras.

Neural radiance fields (NeRF)~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf} and 3D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl3Dgaussians} enable high-quality novel view synthesis but require extensive per-scene optimization and are primarily designed for static scenes.

\subsection{Object Detection and Multi-Object Tracking}

Modern object detectors achieve real-time performance with high accuracy. The YOLO family~\cite{redmon2016yolo} has evolved through multiple versions, with YOLOv8~\cite{yolov8} achieving state-of-the-art speed-accuracy trade-offs. Transformer-based detectors like DETR~\cite{carion2020detr} and its variants offer end-to-end detection but with higher computational cost.

Multi-object tracking (MOT) maintains object identities across video frames. Tracking-by-detection approaches associate detections across frames using appearance and motion cues. SORT~\cite{bewley2016sort} uses Kalman filtering and Hungarian matching for efficient online tracking. DeepSORT~\cite{wojke2017deepsort} adds appearance features for improved re-identification. ByteTrack~\cite{bytetrack} achieves state-of-the-art performance by associating every detection box, including low-confidence ones, effectively recovering objects through occlusions. We adopt ByteTrack for its robustness to partial occlusions common at intersections.

\subsection{Semantic Segmentation}

Semantic segmentation assigns class labels to each pixel. FCN~\cite{long2015fcn} introduced fully convolutional networks for dense prediction. DeepLab variants~\cite{chen2017deeplab,chen2018deeplabv3plus} use atrous convolutions and spatial pyramid pooling. Transformer-based methods like SegFormer~\cite{segformer} achieve strong performance with efficient hierarchical architectures. We employ SegFormer trained on Cityscapes~\cite{cityscapes} to identify road and sidewalk regions for ground plane estimation.

\subsection{Multi-Camera Tracking and 3D Localization}

Multi-camera tracking extends single-camera MOT to associate objects across overlapping views. Graph-based methods~\cite{ristani2018features} model cross-camera associations as graph matching problems. Recent approaches leverage 3D geometry for improved association~\cite{xu2016multi}. Our work differs by focusing on 3D localization for visibility analysis rather than re-identification, using spatial-temporal clustering with ground plane projection for cross-camera association.
