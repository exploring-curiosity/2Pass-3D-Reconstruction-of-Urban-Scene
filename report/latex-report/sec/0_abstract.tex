\begin{abstract}
Pedestrian safety at urban intersections critically depends on visibility between pedestrians and approaching vehicles. We present a two-pass 3D reconstruction system for automated line-of-sight audits at crosswalks. Our method processes synchronized multi-camera video through: (1) static scene reconstruction using Pi3 to establish world geometry and camera parameters from temporal median background images, and (2) dynamic object detection and tracking using YOLOv8x and ByteTrack with monocular depth estimation from Depth Anything V2 for accurate 3D localization. We employ SegFormer semantic segmentation for robust ground plane estimation and perform multi-camera track association to maintain consistent object identities across views. Dynamic objects are represented as canonical 3D primitives---oriented bounding boxes for vehicles and cylinders for pedestrians---enabling efficient visibility analysis. Experiments on a real urban intersection with 8 synchronized cameras demonstrate accurate static reconstruction (1.8 pixel median reprojection error), robust tracking (78.4\% MOTA), and sub-meter 3D localization accuracy (0.42m for vehicles, 0.28m for pedestrians). Our system provides a foundation for automated safety audits that can identify visibility hazards and occlusion sources at pedestrian crossings.
\end{abstract}
